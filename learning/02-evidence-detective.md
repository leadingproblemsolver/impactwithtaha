# Evidence Detective

**A free REF learning mini-workbook | October 2026 | Reader edition 1.0**
**Audience:** Students aged 10–14
**Learning outcome:** Tell apart observation, hypothesis and unanswered question.

## How to use this book
Read the short explanation, attempt its practice before checking the worked answer, then change the example and repeat from memory.

## 1. Classify statements

“The seed grew two centimetres” is a measurement. “The window caused it” is a hypothesis. A good investigator keeps these separate.

**Your practice:** Classify: the paper tore; wet paper tears more easily; does dry paper differ?

**Worked example / answer check:** Observation; hypothesis; question.

**Transfer:** Try a new case from a different topic, without copying the example; write down what you checked.

## 2. Find the original source

Repeated claims can drift from the original. Check where the statement came from, its date and whether you have permission to share it.

**Your practice:** A friend says the timetable changed. What is the next safe check?

**Worked example / answer check:** Read the current official school notice or ask a teacher; do not claim confirmation before checking.

**Transfer:** Try a new case from a different topic, without copying the example; write down what you checked.

## 3. Consider other explanations

One event can have several causes. Design a safe check that distinguishes explanations instead of choosing your favourite.

**Your practice:** A lamp does not light. Suggest two causes and a safe check.

**Worked example / answer check:** It may be unplugged or have a faulty bulb. Ask an adult to inspect safely; never handle damaged wiring.

**Transfer:** Try a new case from a different topic, without copying the example; write down what you checked.

## 4. Confidence does not equal proof

A detailed or confident answer may still be wrong. Ask whether others can check the same source, method and result.

**Your practice:** Rewrite “This study trick always works” after one successful trial.

**Worked example / answer check:** “I recalled five words in one practice session; I have not tested other topics or days.”

**Transfer:** Try a new case from a different topic, without copying the example; write down what you checked.

## Independent challenge
Keep an evidence notebook for one everyday claim: original source, observation, hypothesis, check and remaining uncertainty.

Use this evidence card: **before / action / expected / observed / one limitation / next test**.

## Five-day practice
- Day 1: Try lesson one before reading its example answer.
- Day 2: Recall the core idea with notes closed; check and correct.
- Day 3: Try another lesson with a changed example.
- Day 4: Complete the independent challenge.
- Day 5: Show the result to a suitable reviewer and revise one step based on real feedback.
